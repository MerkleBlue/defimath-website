<h2>What it's for</h2>
<p>The density sits under every curvature-flavoured risk number — gamma, vega,
the decay half of theta — and outside options under maximum-likelihood fits,
kernel estimators, and Bayesian updates on a Gaussian prior. Until now, callers
who wanted <code>φ(x)</code> on its own hand-rolled it from <code>exp</code> and a pasted
<code>0.39894…</code>, which is both slower than a purpose-built routine and easy to get
subtly wrong at the rounding boundary.</p>
<pre class="shiki github-dark" style="background-color:#24292e;color:#e1e4e8" tabindex="0"><code><span class="line"><span style="color:#79B8FF">uint256</span><span style="color:#E1E4E8"> d </span><span style="color:#F97583">=</span><span style="color:#E1E4E8"> Math.</span><span style="color:#B392F0">stdNormPDF</span><span style="color:#E1E4E8">(x);   </span><span style="color:#6A737D">// φ(x) — density</span></span>
<span class="line"><span style="color:#79B8FF">uint256</span><span style="color:#E1E4E8"> p </span><span style="color:#F97583">=</span><span style="color:#E1E4E8"> Math.</span><span style="color:#B392F0">stdNormCDF</span><span style="color:#E1E4E8">(x);   </span><span style="color:#6A737D">// Φ(x) — cumulative</span></span></code></pre>
<p>At 320 gas it costs roughly half of <a href="/docs/math/stdnormcdf/"><code>stdNormCDF</code></a>'s
618 — which tends to surprise people who assume the derivative is the harder of
the two. The <a href="/docs/math/stdnormpdf/">function page</a> explains why, with the
assembly.</p>
<h2>Upgrading</h2>
<p>Drop-in. v4.1.0 is v4.0.0 plus one function: no renames, no signature changes,
and no gas or precision movement anywhere else. Suite totals move to 755
Hardhat tests and 114 Foundry properties; the head-to-head is reproducible in
<a href="https://github.com/MerkleBlue/defimath-compare#readme">defimath-compare</a>.</p>
